🚀Brief Introduction

Mathematical supervision for language models is typically flattened into isolated problem–solution pairs, discarding the curricular organization and evidential structure of the original sources. We introduce TOME-Math (Textbook-Oriented Mathematical Evidence), a large-scale corpus that converts 2,118 textbooks spanning the core areas of university-level mathematics into approximately 380K structured proof-supervision records while preserving chapter order, proposition–proof links, dependency references, and source provenance.

Leveraging these structural signals, we explore curriculum learning across multiple mathematical subjects and characterize scaling patterns with supervision volume and source breadth. At full scale, fine-tuning on TOME-Math raises the unweighted mean score across our held-out evaluations and established public benchmarks by 16.7 percentage points over the base model, while exceeding all three public instruction-data baselines evaluated in aggregate. Our findings highlight structured textbooks as both a scalable source of mathematical supervision and an empirical framework for studying the impact of data organization on mathematical reasoning.

Question

Let \(p_1,p_2,p_3\) be points in the hyperbolic plane \(\mathbb H^2\). Prove the triangle inequality\[d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_1,p_3)\le d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_1,p_2)+d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_2,p_3),\]and prove that equality holds if and only if \(p_2\) lies on the unique hyperbolic shortest path from \(p_1\) to \(p_3\).

Answer

Let \(\gamma_{ij}\) be the hyperbolic shortest path from \(p_i\) to \(p_j\), and set \(\delta=\gamma_{12}*\gamma_{23}\). Then\[l_{\mathbb H^2}(\delta)=l_{\mathbb H^2}(\gamma_{12})+l_{\mathbb H^2}(\gamma_{23})=d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_1,p_2)+d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_2,p_3).\]Since \(\gamma_{13}\) is a shortest path from \(p_1\) to \(p_3\),\[d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_1,p_3)=l_{\mathbb H^2}(\gamma_{13})\le l_{\mathbb H^2}(\delta),\]which proves the triangle inequality. If equality holds, then \(\delta\) is also a shortest path from \(p_1\) to \(p_3\). Uniqueness implies that \(\delta\) and \(\gamma_{13}\) have the same trajectory, so \(p_2\in\gamma_{13}\). Conversely, if \(p_2\) lies on \(\gamma_{13}\), splitting \(\gamma_{13}\) at \(p_2\) gives two shortest subpaths whose lengths add, and equality follows.

Let \(p_1,p_2,p_3\in\mathbb H^2\). For \(1\le i<j\le3\), let \(\gamma_{ij}\) denote the unique hyperbolic shortest path from \(p_i\) to \(p_j\), and let \(\delta=\gamma_{12}*\gamma_{23}\) be their concatenation through \(p_2\). Assume that\[d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_1,p_3)=d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_1,p_2)+d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_2,p_3).\]Prove that \(\delta\) has the same trajectory as \(\gamma_{13}\), and hence that \(p_2\) lies on the hyperbolic shortest path from \(p_1\) to \(p_3\).

Answer

By construction,\[l_{\mathbb H^2}(\delta)=d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_1,p_2)+d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_2,p_3).\]The assumed equality therefore gives \(l_{\mathbb H^2}(\delta)=d_{\mathbb H^2}(p_1,p_3)\). Thus \(\delta\) attains the minimum possible length among all paths from \(p_1\) to \(p_3\), so it is itself a hyperbolic shortest path. The shortest path between two points of \(\mathbb H^2\) is unique up to reparametrization. Hence \(\delta\) and \(\gamma_{13}\) have the same trajectory. The spectrum of a strongly regular graph is determined completely by its four parameters as follows. As \(\lambda\) is used often to signify an eigenvalue, we change the notation \(s r g(n,d,\lambda ,\mu)\) to \(s r g(n,d,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\) to avoid interference. Theorem 2.15 Let \(G\) be a connected strongly regular graph \(s r g(n,d,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\) with \(n\geq 3\) . If \(G\) is neither complete nor empty, then \(\lambda_{1} = d\) is an eigenvalue with multiplicity \(m_{1} = 1\) , and any other eigenvalue \(\lambda (\neq \lambda_{1})\) satisfies\[\lambda^{2} + (\mu_{2} - \mu_{1})\lambda +(\mu_{2} - d) = 0.\]The equation has two distinct solutions \(\lambda_{2}\) and \(\lambda_{3}\) with \(\lambda_{2} > 0 > \lambda_{3}\) , and \(\lambda_{3}\) is an eigenvalue. If \(d + (n - 1)\lambda_{3}\neq 0\) , then \(\lambda_{2}\) is also an eigenvalue. Their multiplicities \(m_{2}\) and \(m_{3}\) can be determined by\[m_{2} + m_{3} = n - 1,\mathrm{and}\ d + m_{2}\lambda_{2} + m_{3}\lambda_{3} = 0.\]Proof. Let \(A\) be the adjacency matrix of \(G\) . Let \(I\) and \(J\) be the \(n\times n\) identity matrix and all-one matrix, respectively. By the definition of \(A\) and the fact that \(A\) is symmetric with zeros on the main diagonal, the \((i,i)\)-entry of \(A^{2}\) is \(d(\nu_{i}) = d\) , which can be represented by \(dI\) . For \(i\neq j\) , the \((i,j)\)-entry of \(A^{2}\) counts common neighbors of vertices \(\nu_{i}\) and \(\nu_{j}\) , so it is \(\mu_{1}\) or \(\mu_{2}\) , which can be represented by \(\mu_{1}A\) or \(\mu_{2}(J - I - A)\) , respectively. Also the regularity of \(G\) can be represented by \(AJ = dJ\) . So \(G\) is an \(s r g(n,d,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\) is equivalent to\[AJ = dJ,\qquad A^{2} = (d - \mu_{2})I + (\mu_{1} - \mu_{2})A + \mu_{2}J.\]From Perron-Frobenius Theorem, we have that \(\lambda_{1} = d\) is an eigenvalue of \(G\) with multiplicity 1 with eigenvector \(\mathbf{1} = (1,1,\ldots ,1)^{T}\) . Let \(\lambda \neq d\) be another eigenvalue of \(G\) and \(x\in \mathbb R^{n}\) a corresponding eigenvector. Thus \(Jx = 0\) by noting \(\mathbf{1}^{T}x = 0\) since eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues of real symmetric matrix are orthogonal. As \(Ax = \lambda x\) and \(A^{2}x = \lambda^{2}x\) , we obtain that\[\lambda^{2}x = (d - \mu_{2})x + (\mu_{1} - \mu_{2})\lambda x,\]which implies that \(\lambda\) must satisfy\[\lambda^{2} + (\mu_{2} - \mu_{1})\lambda +(\mu_{2} - d) = 0. \quad (2.4)\]The equation has two distinct solutions \(\lambda_{2}\) and \(\lambda_{3}\) (\(\lambda_{2} > \lambda_{3}\)). Since the smallest eigenvalue of \(G\) is negative, it follows that \(\lambda_{3}\) is an eigenvalue. Thus, if \(d + (n - 1)\lambda_{3}\neq 0\) , then the multiplicity of \(\lambda_{3}\) cannot be \(n - 1\) , and \(G\) has another eigenvalue, which must be \(\lambda_{2}\) . Also, their multiplicities \(m_{2}\) and \(m_{3}\) satisfy\[1 + m_{2} + m_{3} = n,\qquad \mathrm{Tr}(A) = d + m_{2}\lambda_{2} + m_{3}\lambda_{3} = 0,\]which determine \(m_{2}\) and \(m_{3}\) completely.

Question

Let \(G\) be a connected strongly regular graph with parameters \((n,d,\mu_1,\mu_2)\), where \(n\ge3\), and assume that \(G\) is neither complete nor empty. Prove that \(d\) is an eigenvalue of multiplicity one and that every other eigenvalue satisfies\[\lambda^2+(\mu_2-\mu_1)\lambda+(\mu_2-d)=0.\]If the roots are \(\lambda_2>\lambda_3\), prove that \(\lambda_2>0>\lambda_3\) and that \(\lambda_3\) is an eigenvalue. Show that \(\lambda_2\) is also an eigenvalue whenever \(d+(n-1)\lambda_3\ne0\), and determine the equations satisfied by their multiplicities.

Answer

Let \(A\) be the adjacency matrix of \(G\). Strong regularity gives\[AJ=dJ,\qquad A^2=(d-\mu_2)I+(\mu_1-\mu_2)A+\mu_2J.\]Because \(G\) is connected and \(d\)-regular, the Perron–Frobenius theorem shows that \(d\) is an eigenvalue of multiplicity one with eigenvector \(\mathbf1\). Let \(Ax=\lambda x\) with \(\lambda\ne d\). Since \(A\) is real symmetric, \(x\perp\mathbf1\), and hence \(Jx=0\). Applying the matrix identity to \(x\) yields\[\lambda^2x=(d-\mu_2)x+(\mu_1-\mu_2)\lambda x,\]so \(\lambda\) satisfies the displayed quadratic equation. The equation has two distinct real roots, with the larger root positive. Since \(\operatorname{tr}(A)=0\) while \(d>0\), a negative eigenvalue must occur; therefore \(\lambda_3<0\) and \(\lambda_3\) is an eigenvalue. If \(d+(n-1)\lambda_3\ne0\), the multiplicity of \(\lambda_3\) cannot be \(n-1\), because otherwise the trace would equal that nonzero quantity. Thus the other root \(\lambda_2\) must also occur. If their multiplicities are \(m_2\) and \(m_3\), dimension and trace give\[m_2+m_3=n-1,\qquad d+m_2\lambda_2+m_3\lambda_3=0.\]These two equations determine \(m_2\) and \(m_3\).

Question

Let \(G\) be a connected strongly regular graph with parameters \((n,d,\mu_1,\mu_2)\), and assume that \(G\) is neither complete nor empty. Every nonprincipal eigenvalue of its adjacency matrix is one of the two distinct real roots \(\lambda_2>\lambda_3\) of\[\lambda^2+(\mu_2-\mu_1)\lambda+(\mu_2-d)=0.\]Prove that \(\lambda_3<0\) and that \(\lambda_3\) must occur as an eigenvalue of \(G\).

Answer

The adjacency matrix \(A\) has zero diagonal, so \(\operatorname{tr}(A)=0\) and the sum of its eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity, is zero. Its principal eigenvalue is \(d>0\). If every other eigenvalue were nonnegative, their sum together with \(d\) would be positive, a contradiction. Hence a negative nonprincipal eigenvalue exists. Since every nonprincipal eigenvalue is either \(\lambda_2\) or \(\lambda_3\) and \(\lambda_2>\lambda_3\), it follows that \(\lambda_3<0\). Suppose that \(\lambda_3\) did not occur. Then \(A\) would act as \(\lambda_2 I\) on \(\mathbf1^\perp\) and as multiplication by \(d\) on \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}\). Consequently,\[A=\lambda_2 I+\frac{d-\lambda_2}{n}J.\]All off-diagonal entries of \(A\) would therefore be equal. Because an adjacency matrix has off-diagonal entries only in \(\{0,1\}\), the graph would have to be empty or complete, contradicting the hypothesis. Thus \(\lambda_3\) is an eigenvalue of \(G\).

Moments of Beta Distributions Theorem 5.8.3 Moments. Suppose that \(X\) has the beta distribution with parameters \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) . Then for each positive integer \(k\) ,\[E(X^{k}) = \frac{\alpha(\alpha + 1)\cdot\cdot\cdot(\alpha + k - 1)}{(\alpha + \beta)(\alpha + \beta + 1)\cdot\cdot\cdot(\alpha + \beta + k - 1)}. \quad (5.8.6)\]In particular,\[E(X) = \frac{\alpha}{\alpha + \beta},\]\[\mathrm{Var}(X) = \frac{\alpha\beta}{(\alpha + \beta)^{2}(\alpha + \beta + 1)}.\] Proof For \(k = 1,2,\ldots\)\[E(X^{k}) = \int_{0}^{1}x^{k}f(x|\alpha ,\beta)dx\]\[= \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + \beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha +k - 1}(1 - x)^{\beta -1}dx.\] Therefore, by Eq. (5.8.2),\[E(X^{k}) = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + \beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\cdot \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + k)\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha + k + \beta)},\]which simplifies to Eq. (5.8.6). The special case of the mean is simple, while the variance follows easily from \[E(X^{2}) = \frac{\alpha(\alpha + 1)}{(\alpha + \beta)(\alpha + \beta + 1)}.\]

Question

Let \(X\sim\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)\) with density\[f(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1},\qquad 0<x<1.\]Prove that for every positive integer \(k\),\[E(X^k)=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+k-1)}{(\alpha+\beta)(\alpha+\beta+1)\cdots(\alpha+\beta+k-1)},\]and use this formula to compute \(E(X)\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(X)\).

Answer

Using the density and the beta integral,\[E(X^k)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int_0^1x^{\alpha+k-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1}\,dx\]\[=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+k)\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+k)}.\] The identity \(\Gamma(z+k)=z(z+1)\cdots(z+k-1)\Gamma(z)\) yields the stated moment formula. Taking \(k=1\) and \(k=2\) gives\[E(X)=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta},\qquad E(X^2)=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{(\alpha+\beta)(\alpha+\beta+1)}.\]Finally, \[\operatorname{Var}(X)=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2=\frac{\alpha\beta}{(\alpha+\beta)^2(\alpha+\beta+1)}.\]

Let \(X\sim\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)\), where \(\alpha,\beta>0\). Suppose that\[E(X)=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta},\qquad E(X^2)=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{(\alpha+\beta)(\alpha+\beta+1)}.\]Use \(\operatorname{Var}(X)=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2\) to derive the variance of \(X\).

设函数 \(f\) 在点 \(a\) 的一个邻域内具有直到 \(n\) 阶的导数。令\[T_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k.\]证明带佩亚诺余项的泰勒公式 \[f(x)=T_n(x)+o((x-a)^n),\qquad x\to a.\]

Answer

令 \(R_n(x)=f(x)-T_n(x)\)。只需证明\[\lim_{x\to a}\frac{R_n(x)}{(x-a)^n}=0.\]由 \(T_n\) 的构造，对 \(0\le k\le n-1\) 有 \(R_n^{(k)}(a)=0\)。连续应用 \(n-1\) 次洛必达法则，得到\[\lim_{x\to a}\frac{R_n(x)}{(x-a)^n}=\lim_{x\to a}\frac{R_n^{(n-1)}(x)}{n!(x-a)}.\]而\[R_n^{(n-1)}(x)=f^{(n-1)}(x)-f^{(n-1)}(a)-f^{(n)}(a)(x-a),\]所以\[\frac{R_n^{(n-1)}(x)}{n!(x-a)}=\frac1{n!}\left[\frac{f^{(n-1)}(x)-f^{(n-1)}(a)}{x-a}-f^{(n)}(a)\right].\]括号内第一项在 \(x\to a\) 时趋于 \(f^{(n)}(a)\)，故整个极限为零。因此 \(R_n(x)=o((x-a)^n)\)，即 \[f(x)=T_n(x)+o((x-a)^n).\]

设 \(f\) 在 \(a\) 的邻域内具有直到 \(n\) 阶的导数，并定义 \(R_n(x)=f(x)-T_n(x)\)。已知\[R_n^{(n-1)}(x)=f^{(n-1)}(x)-f^{(n-1)}(a)-f^{(n)}(a)(x-a).\]证明\[\lim_{x\to a}\frac{R_n^{(n-1)}(x)}{n!(x-a)}=0,\]并说明这一步如何完成佩亚诺余项证明中的最后一次极限计算。

Answer

先作代数变形：\[\frac{R_n^{(n-1)}(x)}{n!(x-a)}=\frac1{n!}\left[\frac{f^{(n-1)}(x)-f^{(n-1)}(a)}{x-a}-f^{(n)}(a)\right].\]因为 \(f^{(n-1)}\) 在 \(a\) 处可微，其导数为 \(f^{(n)}(a)\)，所以\[\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f^{(n-1)}(x)-f^{(n-1)}(a)}{x-a}=f^{(n)}(a).\]由极限的线性运算，所求极限等于\[\frac1{n!}\bigl(f^{(n)}(a)-f^{(n)}(a)\bigr)=0.\]此前对 \(R_n(x)/(x-a)^n\) 连续应用 \(n-1\) 次洛必达法则后恰得到上述极限，因此这一步证明了 \(R_n(x)=o((x-a)^n)\)。

Question

设 \((X,T)\) 是拓扑空间，\(A,B\subset X\)。证明集合的内部满足： 1. \(A^\circ\subset A\)； 2. \(X^\circ=X\)； 3. \((A\cap B)^\circ=A^\circ\cap B^\circ\)； 4. \((A^\circ)^\circ=A^\circ\)； 5. 若 \(A\subset B\)，则 \(A^\circ\subset B^\circ\)。 并举例说明 \((A\cup B)^\circ\) 不一定等于 \(A^\circ\cup B^\circ\)。

Answer

(1) 由内部的定义，内点首先属于原集合，所以 \(A^\circ\subset A\)。 (2) \(X\) 是开集，且每个 \(x\in X\) 都有开邻域 \(X\subset X\)，故 \(X^\circ=X\)。 (3) 因 \(A^\circ\cap B^\circ\) 是开集且包含于 \(A\cap B\)，有 \(A^\circ\cap B^\circ\subset(A\cap B)^\circ\)。反之，若 \(x\in(A\cap B)^\circ\)，则存在开集 \(V\) 使 \(x\in V\subset A\cap B\)，所以 \(x\in A^\circ\cap B^\circ\)。因此两者相等。 (4) \(A^\circ\) 是开集，而开集等于其内部，故 \((A^\circ)^\circ=A^\circ\)。 (5) 若 \(x\in A^\circ\)，则存在开集 \(V\) 使 \(x\in V\subset A\subset B\)，所以 \(x\in B^\circ\)。 最后在 \(\mathbb R\) 的通常拓扑中取 \(A=[0,1]\)、\(B=(1,2]\)。则 \(A^\circ=(0,1)\)、\(B^\circ=(1,2)\)，而 \((A\cup B)^\circ=(0,2)\)。点 \(1\) 属于前者但不属于 \(A^\circ\cup B^\circ\)，故二者不一定相等。
